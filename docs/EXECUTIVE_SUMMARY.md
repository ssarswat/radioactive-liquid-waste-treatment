# Executive summary

The historical experimental programme evaluated ion-exchange treatment of low-level
radioactive aqueous waste using zeolite 4A, 13X-CFC, HMO-PU and CFC-PU.

The central engineering lesson is that **maximum batch adsorption is not equivalent
to best plant-scale choice**. CFC-PU produced very strong batch Kd values, but the
thesis identified fines and attrition as practical risks for once-through columns.
Zeolites were therefore selected for further column work.

Laboratory parallel-column data extended to 6500 bed volumes. The pilot configuration
used 4A followed by 13X-CFC in 50 L columns, with pre-filtration and differential
pressure monitoring. The thesis reports that 4A exhausted at approximately 10,000
bed volumes while 13X-CFC remained effective to approximately 22,000 bed volumes.

The downstream system is part of the engineering problem: spent zeolite was removed
through fluidisation, encapsulated in cement and evaluated for leaching and mechanical
strength.

The resulting repository reframes the dissertation as a reproducible engineering
analytics pipeline rather than a static document.
